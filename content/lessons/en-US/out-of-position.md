# Playing out of position

Out of [position](term:position) you act first on every street: you give information before you get it. That has a cost, and good play reduces it.

## Check a lot, but not only weak hands

The player out of position checks most of the time on the flop. The important part: some strong hands must check too. If you bet all your strong hands and check only weak ones, the opponent can bet every time you check.

## Leading (donk betting)

Betting into the preflop raiser is called a lead or donk bet. In solver strategies it is rare and specific: mostly on turns and rivers that change the board in your favor, like a fourth straight card after you called on a connected flop. On the flop, check almost always.

## Realizing equity

Out of position you realize less of your equity: you are bluffed off hands more often and get less value from strong ones. That is why the big blind defends with hands that play well: suited cards, connected cards, pairs. A hand with 35% equity and no way to continue on most turns is weaker than the number says.

## Smaller pots

Prefer smaller pots out of position with medium hands. Check-call instead of betting; avoid building a pot you cannot control.

## In Taful

Many of your spots as the big blind or small blind are out of position. Watch how often the reference strategy checks, and which strong hands it checks with: that balance is what keeps your [range](term:range) defended.
