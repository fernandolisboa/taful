# Value betting the river

On the river there are no more cards. A bet now is either a [value bet](term:value-bet), made to get called by worse hands, or a bluff, made to fold better hands.

## The value test

Ask: if I bet and get called, am I ahead more than half of the time? If yes, the bet makes money when called. If the opponent only calls with better hands, the bet loses, even if your hand is strong in absolute terms.

## Thin value

A [thin value](term:thin-value) bet is a bet with a hand that is only slightly better than the hands that call. Second pair after a passive line, or top pair with a weak kicker against a player who calls a lot. Thin value is where much of the profit in low-stakes games comes from.

## Sizing for value

- **Big sizes** with the strongest hands: when your range is strong and the opponent's is capped, a pot-size bet or even an overbet gets more from the hands that will call.
- **Small sizes** for thin value: they get called by more worse hands.

## When to check

Check hands with [showdown value](term:showdown-value) that are unlikely to get called by worse: a medium pair after the opponent called two streets. Checking wins at showdown against bluffs and loses less against value.

## Reading the opponent's range

Ask what they would have done with each hand on each street. A player who checks back the turn rarely has the nuts; a player who called flop and turn on a connected board has many draws, and some of them missed. Value bets aim at the hands they still hold, not at the hands you wish they had.
