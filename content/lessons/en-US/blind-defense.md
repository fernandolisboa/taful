# Defending the big blind

The [big blind](term:big-blind) has already paid 1 bb, closes the preflop action, and gets the best price at the table. That is why it defends more hands than any other seat.

## The price

The button opens to 2.5 bb and the small blind folds. The big blind calls 1.5 bb to win a pot of 4 bb, so it needs {{ calc: div(a=1.5, b=5.5) | pct(1) }} equity at showdown. Almost any two cards have that against a 39% button range, but playing out of position costs some of it.

## How much to defend

Taful's reference chart in the big blind:

- **Vs a button open:** folds about 40%, calls about 47%, [3-bets](term:three-bet) about 13%.
- **Vs a cutoff open:** folds about 52%.
- **Vs an UTG open:** folds about 68%. A tight range deserves respect.
- **Vs a small blind open (3 bb):** folds only about 20%.

## What to defend with

- **Suited hands of almost any kind:** they flop flushes, draws and backdoors.
- **Connected offsuit hands:** T9o, 98o, 87o against late opens.
- **Weak offsuit hands like K4o or Q3o** are folds against everything but the widest opens: they make dominated top pairs.

## 3-bet or call?

From the big blind, 3-bet to about 9.5 bb with value hands and a polarized set of bluffs (suited aces, some suited kings). Call with the rest of the defending range. The SB raise or fold strategy means the big blind often faces the small blind alone: defend widely there.

## After the flop

You are out of position, so you will not win every pot you could. Defend with hands that can realize equity, and do not feel obliged to win every flop: check and fold your misses.
