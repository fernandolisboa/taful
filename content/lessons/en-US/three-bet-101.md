# The 3-bet

A [3-bet](term:three-bet) is the first re-raise before the flop (the blinds count as the first bet, the open as the second).

## Value and bluffs

You 3-bet for value with hands that want to build the pot: QQ+, AK, and against wide opens also hands like JJ, TT and AQ. You 3-bet as a bluff with hands that do well when called but are happy to win the pot right away.

Good bluff 3-bets usually share two traits:

- **[Blockers](term:blocker).** An ace in your hand removes combos of AA and AK from the opener's range, so they have fewer hands that continue. A5s and A4s are the classic examples.
- **Playability.** Suited hands make flushes and straights when called: A5s, K9s, 76s.

## Polarized or linear?

A [polarized](term:polarized) 3-bet range has strong hands and bluffs, and calls with the middle. A [linear](term:linear) range 3-bets the best hands top-down and folds the rest.

- Against a player who folds too much to 3-bets, both work.
- When you can call (button, big blind), the middle hands go into calls: the 3-bet range gets polarized.
- When calling is bad (small blind, or a squeeze spot), you 3-bet more linearly: good hands 3-bet, the rest fold.

## Sizing

In position, 3 times the open (to 7.5 bb against 2.5 bb). Out of position, about 3.75 times (to 9.5 bb). Bigger 3-bets out of position cut the opener's pot odds and make the pot easier to play with a lower SPR.
