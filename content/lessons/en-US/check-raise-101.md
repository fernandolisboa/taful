# The check-raise

A [check-raise](term:check-raise) is a check followed by a raise after the opponent bets. It is the main way the player out of position fights back.

## Where it works

The big blind check-raises most on boards that hit its range: low and middle connected flops (8-6-5, 9-7-4 two-tone), where it has more sets, two-pairs and straights. There it has a [nut advantage](term:nut-advantage) even though it was the caller before the flop.

On high, dry boards (A-K-3, K-Q-2) the preflop raiser has the strongest hands. Check-raising there is rare.

## What to check-raise

- **Value:** sets, two-pairs, strong top pairs on wet boards, straights.
- **[Semi-bluffs](term:semi-bluff):** flush draws, open-ended straight draws, combo draws, gutshots with overcards.
- Hands that block the bettor's strongest calls help too.

Medium pairs usually call: raising turns them into a bluff, because only better hands continue.

## Sizing

A check-raise is often about three times the c-bet. Against small c-bets it can be bigger in relation, because the pot is still small.

## Facing a check-raise

As the c-bettor, you face a strong, polarized range. Continue with strong hands and good draws; fold the weakest part of the range that bet small. Do not call down with one pair out of habit.
