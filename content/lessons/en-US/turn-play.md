# Turn play and barreling

You bet the flop and got called. Bet the turn again (a second [barrel](term:barrel)) or check?

## The caller's range is stronger now

The opponent folded their weakest hands on the flop. Their [range](term:range) on the turn is narrower and stronger, so you need a stronger range to keep betting. Most players bet the turn less often than the flop, and bigger.

## Good turn cards to barrel

- **Cards that favor your range:** an ace or king when you raised preflop and the defender has few of them.
- **Cards that complete few of the caller's draws** but give you new ones.
- **Overcards to the board** that scare the caller's middle pairs.

## Bad turn cards to barrel

- Cards that complete the obvious draws the caller has (the third suited card, the straight card on a connected board), unless you hold that draw.
- Low cards that pair the caller's likely holdings.

## Which hands bet

- **Value:** hands that are still well ahead of a calling range: top pair with a good kicker and better.
- **[Semi-bluffs](term:semi-bluff):** draws with good equity, especially ones that become the nuts.
- **Give up** with hands that have no equity and no blockers. Not every flop bluff should continue.

## The delayed c-bet

If you checked behind on the flop, betting the turn is a delayed c-bet. It works well when the opponent checks to you again: their range often has many weak hands.

## Checking back

Checking back in position with medium hands controls the pot and still reaches the river. Your checks should not be only weak hands.
