# Facing an open raise

Someone opened before you. You have three options: fold, call (a [cold call](term:cold-call)), or re-raise (a [3-bet](term:three-bet)).

## Respect the opener's seat

An UTG open is a strong [range](term:range), about 17% of hands. A button open is about 39%. Your continuing range must tighten against early opens and widen against late ones.

## Call or 3-bet?

Outside the big blind, modern 6-max strategy leans on 3-bets and calls rarely:

- **Behind you are the blinds.** A cold call invites a squeeze, and the blinds can join the pot cheaply.
- **A 3-bet can win now.** The opener folds often and you collect the pot without a flop.
- **The initiative carries on.** The 3-bettor is the one expected to bet the flop.

Taful's chart, facing an UTG open from the hijack, folds about 90%, calls about 3% and 3-bets about 6%. From the button against a cutoff open it calls about 6% and 3-bets about 12%.

## Who calls more?

- The **button** calls more: it has position for the whole hand.
- The **big blind** calls the most: it already has 1 bb in, closes the action, and gets the best price.
- The **small blind** mostly 3-bets or folds, because it plays out of position against two players.

## Sizing

3-bets in Taful's tree are 3× the open in position and 3.75× out of position, plus one more open size for each caller. Out of position you need a bigger raise because you will be at a disadvantage after the flop.
