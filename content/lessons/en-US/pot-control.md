# Medium hands and pot control

Most hands you hold after the flop are neither strong nor weak: a middle pair, top pair with a weak kicker, a weak overpair on a wet board. They have [showdown value](term:showdown-value) but cannot happily play for stacks.

## The goal

Get to showdown cheaply when behind, and win a small-to-medium pot when ahead. Building a huge pot with a medium hand means you will mostly get called by better.

## Tools

- **Check back in position** on one street. You see a free card and keep the pot small.
- **Check-call out of position** instead of betting: you face bluffs and keep the opponent's weaker hands in.
- **Small bets** for thin value and protection, instead of big ones that only get called by better.

## SPR and medium hands

With a low [SPR](term:spr) (3-bet and 4-bet pots), top pair is strong enough to get the stacks in. With a high SPR, the same hand becomes a medium hand: plan for one or two streets of value, not three.

## Protection

On wet boards, a medium hand can still want to bet to charge draws. On dry boards, there is little to protect against: check more.

## The balance

Pot control is not passivity. If you always check medium hands and bet only strong ones and bluffs, your checks become weak and opponents attack them. Mix in some strong hands when you check.
