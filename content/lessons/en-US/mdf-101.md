# Minimum defense frequency

[Pot odds](term:pot-odds) look at one hand. The [minimum defense frequency](term:mdf) (MDF) looks at your whole range: how often you must continue so that an opponent's bluffs do not profit automatically.

## The formula

MDF = pot / (pot + bet), with the pot measured before the bet.

- Against a third-pot bet: {{ calc: mdf(pot=3, bet=1) | pct(0) }}.
- Against a half-pot bet: {{ calc: mdf(pot=2, bet=1) | pct(0) }}.
- Against a pot-size bet: {{ calc: mdf(pot=1, bet=1) | pct(0) }}.
- Against a 2× pot overbet: {{ calc: mdf(pot=1, bet=2) | pct(0) }}.

## Why it works

If you fold more often than 1 − MDF, your opponent can bet any two cards as a bluff and make money, because the fold equity alone pays for the bet. Defending at least MDF takes that free profit away.

## Use it with care

MDF is a guide, not a rule. It assumes your opponent bluffs at the right frequency and that every hand in your range is a candidate to continue. Against players who rarely bluff, folding more than MDF is correct; against players who bluff too much, defend more.
