# Range and nut advantage

Before you look at your own two cards, ask: whose [range](term:range) does this flop favor?

## Range advantage

A player has a [range advantage](term:range-advantage) when their whole range has more equity on the board than the opponent's. The button opens 39% and the big blind calls. On A-K-4 rainbow, the button has many more aces and kings: AA, KK, AK and every strong Ax that the big blind would have 3-bet. The button has the range advantage.

On 7-6-5 two-tone, the big blind has more sets of 5s, 6s and 7s, more two-pairs and more straights (98, 84, 43s, …). The range advantage shifts toward the defender.

## Nut advantage

A [nut advantage](term:nut-advantage) is about the very top of the range: who has more of the strongest hands? A player can have a range advantage without a nut advantage, or the reverse.

- **Range advantage, no nut advantage:** bet small and often. You win the pot with many hands, but you do not want to build a huge pot.
- **Nut advantage:** bet big. Your strongest hands want to get the stacks in, and your bluffs are believable because the opponent cannot have those hands.
- **Neither:** check more. Let the opponent bet into you.

## Using it

1. Picture both ranges before the flop.
2. Ask who has more top pairs and better.
3. Ask who has more sets, two-pairs and straights.
4. Choose how often and how big to bet for your whole range, then place your hand in it.
