# Playing the small blind

The small blind is the hardest seat at the table. It has half a big blind in, but it will be out of [position](term:position) against everyone after the flop, and the big blind still acts behind it.

## Facing an open: 3-bet or fold

Calling an open from the small blind invites the big blind to squeeze, and leaves you in the worst position in a three-way pot. So the small blind mostly [3-bets](term:three-bet) or folds.

Taful's chart, with the small blind facing a button open: fold about 77%, call about 10%, 3-bet about 13%. The 3-bet range is fairly [linear](term:linear): strong hands top-down plus some suited hands that play well.

## When it folds to you

When everyone folds to the small blind, it is a blind-versus-blind battle. Taful's chart raises to 3 bb with about 44% of hands and folds the rest: no limps in the reference strategy. The big blind then defends about 80% of the time, so your open has to make money after the flop too.

## After the flop

You act first on every street. That makes the small blind the seat where you check most often, and where your range needs some strong hands in its checks so the opponent cannot bet freely.

## Expectations

The small blind loses money in the long run for every player: it pays a blind each orbit and plays out of position. The goal is to lose as little as possible. A tight, mostly raise-or-fold strategy does exactly that.
