# Hand rankings

At the [showdown](term:showdown), the best five-card hand wins. But what is "best"? There is a fixed order, the same at every table in the world. The rule behind it: the harder a hand is to make, the stronger it is.

From strongest to weakest:

```cards
Straight flush: 9s 8s 7s 6s 5s
Four of a kind: Qh Qd Qc Qs | 4d
Full house: Kc Kh Kd | 7s 7c
Flush: Ah Jh 8h 5h 2h
Straight: Td 9c 8h 7s 6d
Three of a kind: 5c 5h 5s | Ac Jd
Two pair: Ad As | 9h 9c | Kh
One pair: Jh Js | Ac 8d 4c
High card: As Qd 9c 6h 3s
```

1. **Straight flush**: five cards in a row, all of the same suit. The highest, A K Q J T of one suit, is called a royal flush.
2. **Four of a kind**: four cards of the same rank.
3. **Full house**: three of a kind and a pair together.
4. **Flush**: five cards of the same suit, in any order.
5. **Straight**: five ranks in a row, with mixed suits. The ace can be the highest card (A K Q J T) or the lowest (5 4 3 2 A).
6. **Three of a kind**: three cards of the same rank.
7. **Two pair**: two different pairs.
8. **One pair**: two cards of the same rank.
9. **High card**: none of the above. The highest card counts.

The T is the ten: in poker it is written with a single letter, like the other cards.

## Same category: who wins?

When two players have the same category, the ranks decide: a pair of kings beats a pair of queens, and an ace-high flush beats a king-high flush. If it is still a tie, the other cards of the five are compared, from the highest down. The card that breaks the tie is the [kicker](term:kicker).

```cards
Board: Ah 8c 6d 4s 2c
You: As Kd
Opponent: Ac Qh
```

Both of you have a pair of aces, using the ace on the [board](term:board). But your king is higher than their queen: you win on the kicker.

> Suits never break ties: hearts are worth no more than spades. If two players' five cards have the same ranks, the hand is a tie and the pot is shared (a [split pot](term:split-pot)).
